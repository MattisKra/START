In game 24, original Fable opened the photo after its first decision. It cited a premium bicycle and frame scuffing, raised its frame charge from €900 to €1,000 and its limit from €1,500 to €2,000. This cost at least €1,600 net. In game 25 it opened the photo after writing and kept its prices. In game 26 it read the policy before its only decision and never opened the photo.

On the game 24 frame, Terra assumed a like-for-like frame and called for confirmation that replacement was necessary. Sol also distinguished impact damage from cosmetic damage. Its market check found no matching specification or price. The ensemble’s final frame limit was €785.40. Fable’s saved explanation gives no calculation connecting the visible scuffing to its €500 limit increase. The logs establish each reader’s stated assumptions and leave the frame’s actual repairability open.

The tool-enabled companion was frozen before game 25. Both mechanical checks passed, and both checks were read. In game 25 Fable searched the policy before deciding, then inspected the photo and searched the policy again. In game 26 its first logged policy search and read came **after** its only decision. It never opened the photo there. Neither companion used WebSearch or WebFetch, and neither revised. Tool availability alone explains no measured improvement.

Python’s selective Sol reader logged three search actions in game 24. Its rationale rejected a €434.90 frame as an unsuitable comparable. In game 25 the reader was still pending at the deadline, so its tool activity is unknown. In game 26 it logged a search, a page open and a find on page. Its locksmith estimate cited a Munich tariff as an imperfect proxy and allowed half an hour of separately payable work. The revision effects above include the reader’s entire estimate; they cannot be attributed to the lookup alone.

## Holding estimates or strategy fixed

The original Fable receipts contain actions only. A separate game 24 rerun supplied explicit estimates after settlement. On that rerun, Python’s factors improved net by €3,024–€15,201 versus Fable’s chosen actions. Holding the factors fixed, Fable’s estimates versus Python’s ranged from −€2,890 to +€7,041 net. Game 24 is excluded from the forward comparisons below. [Game 24 rerun and all 13 line results](08-fable-price-policy-replay.md)

The companion recorded a conditional gross median and payable probability per line in games 25–26. The factor rule charges 0.90 × median and accepts up to 0.55 × median when invalid probability is below one third, otherwise zero. **Strategy effect** applies this rule to Fable’s estimates and compares with its chosen actions. **Estimator effect** compares Fable’s estimates with Python’s under that same rule.

**Game 25.** On three senior installer hours, Fable allowed €340. The factor limit of €156.75 cost €1,095.89 more on review, outweighing the issuer gain. On the thermal imaging camera, cutting the limit from €220 to €88 saved €440–€814 on review. Both strategies set zero limits on the ten façade and painting positions. Their remaining euro exposure comes from the issuer side.

**Game 26.** The recovered bicycle was undamaged. Both strategies set zero limits on the eight elective bicycle-shop positions. At the locksmith, tightening the cutting fee limit from €120 to €49.50 cost €299.70 on review. Tightening the separate skilled hour from €100 to €42.90 saved €355.66. Fable priced the cutting as two units and allowed an additional hour. The Python readers disagreed over whether the cutting fee already included that labour. Terra’s two readers allowed one locksmith hour at €95–€100 net per hour and put payability at 90–95%. Sol allowed half an hour at €85–€100 net per hour and put payability at 25–60%, citing the potential duplicate under clause 7.1.8(g). The rates overlap. The disagreement concerns separately payable time. On the bicycle shop’s identically named position 8, all four readers judged the work excluded. The settlements identify the monetary effect of each choice but leave actual attendance unknown.

The evidence supports evaluating limits separately from issuer pricing. It does not establish a universal multiplier, an estimator winner on total net, or a causal benefit from web access.
