# Hybrid reviewer · prospective games 26–28

**The hybrid used the factor on all 16 printed positions and saved €0 against always using it.** Always using Fable’s limit saved €2,484.63–€3,296.62 against the hybrid across the block. Seven positions had distinct candidate limits. Only one final decision distinguished the three selectors. Label-only raised the limit on a locksmith hour and paid €355.66 more. Vote-only and hybrid agreed on all final limits.

The factor sets the limit to 0.55 × Fable’s conditional gross median when its estimated probability of invalidity is below one third, and zero otherwise. Always-Fable uses Fable’s chosen limit. Label-only uses that limit for a printed hourly unit or assembly description when Fable passes the validity test. Vote-only requires two model votes based on payability and which candidate limit is closer to the reader’s median. The hybrid also requires each supporting reader to classify the work as hourly or assembly.

## What the block supports

At either cutoff, hybrid and label-only agreed on **6/7 (85.7%)** lines with distinct candidates, vote-only and label-only agreed on **6/7**, and hybrid and vote-only agreed on **7/7**. The nine equal-limit lines and repeated observations are excluded from these fractions. The provisional prediction of at least 90% hybrid/label agreement missed on this block by one decision.

The predeclared minimum was five hybrid switches by 54 seconds. There were **zero**, including across intermediate receipts. There was one final ablation disagreement, and four distinct lines with temporary vote-only switches. The votes prevented one €355.66 loss on labelled work. There is insufficient forward evidence to adopt the hybrid, to claim that voting generally improves decisions, or to choose the best time to stop collecting votes.
