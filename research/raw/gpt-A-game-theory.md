# Claim to Fame — game theory and transferable methods

## TL;DR

1. **Build one posterior for the expert's fair value, then score `a` and `b` separately.** Start with coverage, relation to damage, and the gross line total. Preserve a probability mass at zero for exclusions or unrelated work. This is the largest shared input to both decisions. **High confidence in the structure, medium in the winning edge.** [Rules and files, slides 2–6](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
2. **Use the cap-aware insurer rule.** For a positive charge `y`, rejecting saves money when `P(T<y | information) > y / (y + 2 min(y,c))`. The threshold is **1/3 when `y ≤ c`**, **1/2 at `y=2c`**, and rises further for larger charges. The API takes one `b`, so minimize the cost over all charges that this limit would accept. **High.** [Settlement rules, slides 3–4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
3. **Choose `a` by expected receipts over a small legal price grid.** Fair charges earn more against opponents who reject. Inflated charges earn only when accepted, with a cap. Compare both effects before shading a price down. **High for the rule, medium for estimated inputs.** [Settlement rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
4. **Use the pure minimax pair as a benchmark when opponent behavior is unknown.** On a shared finite price grid and under a common public posterior, maximize the smaller of the expected accepted and rejected payments to set `a`. Set `b` to the largest charge with cheaper acceptance than rejection, counting ties. This pair guarantees nonnegative **expected** net score against any opponent actions in that model. **High under the stated assumptions.** [Proof below](#pure-saddle-on-a-shared-grid) · [Settlement rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
5. **Establish the feedback before choosing a learning algorithm.** Opponent limits, acceptance bits, fairness labels, per-pair payments, and net leaderboard deltas identify different quantities. An accepted charge under the cap pays the same whether fair or inflated. **High.** [Settlement and API, slides 4 and 7](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md) · [Turnbull 1976](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/38/3/290/7027379)
6. **Pool observed opponent behavior by case type and price relative to the fair-value estimate.** Start with pooled estimates and add team-level effects only if they improve held-out payment predictions. The same hidden fair value applies to all teams on a line, so validate by case or round. **Medium.** [Kleinberg–Leighton 2003](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rdk/papers/oppa.pdf) · [Javanmard–Nazerzadeh 2019](https://jmlr.org/papers/v20/17-357.html) · [Rules, slides 3–4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
7. **Make an on-time, correctly indexed submission a prerequisite for strategy experiments.** An omitted line gives up all receipts and still pays 1.5× for every positive fair charge. **High for the failure cost.** [Submission and scoring, slides 6–8](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

## Game theory and exact best responses

### Published structure and interpretation

**Published result.** In a finite zero-sum polymatrix game, each player sums payoffs from pairwise games and the total payoff is zero. Cai et al. find Nash equilibria by linear programming and show that the player marginals of a coarse correlated equilibrium form a Nash equilibrium. A general zero-sum polymatrix game's equilibrium payoffs can vary. Harsanyi gives the Bayesian formulation when players receive private signals about the game. **Confidence high.** [Cai et al. 2016, Theorems 1–2](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/cai/publication/cai-zero-sum-2016/cai-zero-sum-2016.pdf) · [Harsanyi 1967](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.14.3.159)

**Interpretation.** For a line, write `X` for the information available before submitting, `T` for the hidden fair value, `c` for the payment cap, and `P(a,b;T,c)` for the payment from one insurer to one handyman. Team `i` receives stage score `u_i = Σ_{j≠i} [P(a_i,b_j;T,c) − P(a_j,b_i;T,c)]`. Every unordered team pair contributes opposite scores to its two members, so realized total score is zero. Each round can contain multiple lines. The public schedule implies roughly 100 rounds, with one minute to submit each case. [Game spec](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/research/raw/GAME_SPEC.md) · [Organizer slides, 3–9](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

**Derived, high confidence.** Conditional on the same information, fixed opponent strategies, and the stated line-wise cap, `E[u_i | X] = R_i(a_i) − C_i(b_i)`. Maximize receipts and minimize payments independently. This uses linearity, so dependence between `T` and opponents' actions, or between the two parts of your realized score, is allowed. A mean score margin against one fixed rival also separates, with twice the weight on direct transfers with that rival. Maximizing probability of first place or optimizing future reactions, information, or a joint budget can couple decisions. The actual cap, accepted amount bounds and precision, available settlement feedback, and detailed judging criteria remain **unverified** from the supplied materials. The example in slide 5 implies `c ≥ 120` for that example. [Rules, slides 3–5 and 9](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Insurer — why one-third is conditional

Define `y>0` as a rival charge, `q_j(y)=P(T≥y | X,A_j=y)`, and `m(y)=min(y,c)`. A rival's charge can carry information about the fair value if it reflects evidence missing from `X`. Expected costs if that charge could be treated separately are:

| Action | Expected cost |
| --- | --- |
| Accept | `U_j(y)=y q_j(y)+m(y)[1−q_j(y)]` |
| Reject | `D_j(y)=1.5 y q_j(y)` |
| Accept instead of reject | `Δ_j(y)=U_j(y)−D_j(y)=m(y)[1−q_j(y)]−0.5 y q_j(y)` |

**Derived, high confidence.** Strictly prefer rejection iff `Δ>0`, equivalently `p=P(T<y | X,A_j=y)> y/[y+2m(y)]`. Equality is a cost tie. Charge zero always pays zero. Fairness includes `y=T`. If the cap is uncertain, use `E[min(y,C) 1{T<y} | X,A_j=y]` for the inflated-acceptance term until the cap is known. [Settlement rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md) · [Elkan 2001](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2001/133)

**Exact rule for one shared `b`.** Minimize `C_i(b)=Σ_j E[D_j(A_j) | X]+Σ_j E[1{A_j≤b} Δ_j(A_j) | X]`. With weighted empirical or simulated charges, sort by charge, group equal charges, and choose the prefix with the smallest cumulative change. Start with `b=0`, which rejects all positive charges. For a joint draw of actual `T,A_j`, the prefix contribution is `−0.5 A_j` if fair and `min(A_j,c)` if inflated. This estimates the same objective without fitting a separate conditional classifier. [Settlement and submitted limit, slides 3–4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

**When a quantile works.** If the cap is inactive over relevant charges and there is a common posterior `P(T | X)` independent of the incoming charge conditional on `X`, the best decisions switch at the lower one-third quantile. On a grid, a safe description is to include all positive prices with `P(T<y | X)≤1/3`, using the largest such feasible price and respecting ties and support. If `T=0` is certain and `c>0`, `b=0` is optimal. With a binding cap or informative opponent charges, `Δ` can change sign multiple times. Example with `c=100` and `T=250` with probability 0.6 (otherwise zero): at `y=100`, accept costs 100 and reject costs 90. At `y=200`, accept costs 160 and reject costs 180. With one charge at each amount, the smallest expected total cost is 260 at `b=200`. Rejecting both costs 270, and accepting only 100 costs 280. [Derived from slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Handyman — fair benchmark and when inflation pays

**Exact rule.** For a candidate `a`, let `F={T≥a}` and `S_j={B_j≥a}`. Score `R_i(a)=Σ_j {a P(F∩S_j) + 1.5a P(F∩S_jᶜ) + min(a,c) P(Fᶜ∩S_j)}` conditional on `X`. Maximize over legal gross totals. Evaluate joint scenarios if fair-value and limit estimates share market-price or coverage signals. If independent given `X`, use `q=P(F|X)` and `s_j=P(S_j|X)` to get `a q(1.5−0.5s_j)+min(a,c)(1−q)s_j` per opponent. Increasing `s_j` raises receipts when `min(a,c)(1−q)>0.5 a q` and lowers receipts when the inequality reverses. **Confidence high.** [Derived from slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

**Known `t`.** The largest permitted fair charge is the best fair charge. Raising a fair charge increases the price and can only increase the fraction of opponents rejecting it. When `t` is feasible, compare the fair receipt per opponent `t[1+0.5(1−s(t))]` against `min(a,c)s(a)` for each `a>t`, where `s(a)` averages the opponents' acceptance probabilities. Inflation strictly beats the fair charge when the latter expression is larger. With `t=100`, `c=200`, and every opponent using `b=140`, charging 140 earns 140 per opponent versus 100 at `a=t`. If `c≤t` and `t` is feasible, an inflated charge cannot strictly beat `a=t`. With `t=0`, positive receipts are possible only via accepted inflation. The hackathon's detailed judging treatment of deliberately inflated charges is **unverified**. [Derived from slides 3–5 and 9](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

**Two diagnostics.** If all opponents reject positive charges, choose `argmax_a a P(T≥a | X)`. For continuous `T` with density `f`, an interior optimum with positive survival satisfies `P(T≥a)=a f(a)`. If all accept and `a≤c), receipts per opponent equal `a` regardless of `T`. Thus pricing at a fixed posterior quantile need not maximize receipts. A cap on inflated accepted payments still allows a fair charge or penalty above `c`. [Derived from slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Pure saddle on a shared grid

**Derived result, high confidence under the assumptions.** For one line, suppose everyone has the same public posterior of `T`, current actions carry no additional information about `T`, the cap `c≥0` is fixed, and `a,b` share a finite feasible grid containing zero. For the row player (handyman) and column player (insurer), let `U(y),D(y)` be the common expected accept and reject payments above with `q(y)=P(T≥y|X)`. Then:

```text
a* ∈ argmax_y min(U(y), D(y))
b* = max { y : U(y) ≤ D(y) }
v  = max_y min(U(y), D(y))
```

**Proof.** Charging `a*` guarantees expected receipts at least `v`. At any positive price `y`, acceptance and rejection cost the same when `q=2m(y)/(y+2m(y))`. Their common cost `κ(y)=3 y m(y)/(y+2m(y))` is nondecreasing in `y` for a fixed cap. Set `κ(0)=0`. For each `y>b*`, rejection costs `min(U,D)≤v`. For `y≤b*` with `U≤D`, acceptance also costs `min(U,D)≤v`. The remaining prices satisfy `U(y)>D(y)` and hence `U(y)≤κ(y)≤κ(b*)≤U(b*)≤v`. Thus the single threshold `b*` delivers maximum expected payment at most `v`, even if the cheapest per-charge action switches more than once. [Derived from slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

The proof also covers a common closed, bounded price interval containing zero. Because equality is fair, `q(y)=P(T≥y)` includes any atom at `y` and can jump down only immediately to its right. Thus `q` is upper semicontinuous. So are `min(U,D)` and `D−U=(0.5y+m(y))q(y)−m(y)`. The objective attains its maximum, and the closed set where `D−U≥0` has a largest element. [Derived from slides 3–4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

**Interpretation.** One team deploying both roles guarantees at least zero conditional expected stage score against any fixed or randomized opponent actions under these information assumptions. If all teams deploy the pair, each is best responding. In any stage Nash equilibrium of this model, every team must receive zero expected net score because each can unilaterally guarantee zero and total score is zero. When `t` is known and feasible, `(a,b)=(t,t)` is a saddle with per-transfer value `t`. This follows since no charge can earn more than `t` against `b=t`, and `a=t` earns at least `t` against any limit. [Derived from slides 3–4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md) · [Cai et al. 2016](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/cai/publication/cai-zero-sum-2016/cai-zero-sum-2016.pdf)

**Transfer hypothesis, medium confidence.** Use this pair as the opponent-agnostic benchmark and compare it with best responses to observed behavior. For a known case posterior and fixed opponent policies, the sum of these stage objectives is the expected tournament score. With private signals, a Bayesian Nash equilibrium requires both best-response conditions for each team's signal under the beliefs induced by all teams' policies. Private signals can associate opponent actions with `T`, which invalidates the common-posterior guarantee. The joint distribution of team signals and the feedback that drives future adaptation are **unverified**. [Game spec](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/research/raw/GAME_SPEC.md) · [Harsanyi 1967](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.14.3.159)

**Verification.** Replaying the organizer example reproduced Alpha +30, Delta +150, and Beta −180. Exact rational arithmetic checked the pure saddle, prefix cost, and rejection inequality on 2,000 generated grids with arbitrary `q(y)`, including zero and binding caps. [Rules and example, slides 4–5](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

## Transfer from other fields

### Inventory control and quantile regression

- **Published, high.** The single-period newsvendor minimizes expected asymmetric overage and underage cost at a demand quantile. For loss `u(T−z)₊+o(z−T)₊`, the critical fractile is `u/(u+o)`. Koenker and Bassett extend this asymmetric absolute loss to conditional quantile estimation. [Arrow, Harris & Marschak 1951](https://www.or.mist.i.u-tokyo.ac.jp/takeda/FreshmanCourse/ArrowHarrisMarschak.pdf) · [Koenker & Bassett 1978](https://www.bls.gov/osmr/research-papers/1992/ec921690.htm) · [Gneiting 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169207010000063)
- **Interpretation, high.** Under the simple uncapped insurer model, each marginal euro of limit changes the decision on charges near that price. The wrong accept costs `y`, and the extra loss from rejecting a fair `y` is `0.5y`. This gives the same one-third cutoff on `P(T<y)`. The actual score depends on each price and the opponent charge distribution. [Derived from slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, medium.** Train or calibrate conditional fair-value quantiles if labels exist. Use the lower one-third quantile as an insurer baseline, then use the entire fitted distribution for the cap-aware prefix and handyman grid. Select `a` by expected payments, with low quantiles serving as candidate prices. [Koenker & Bassett 1978](https://www.bls.gov/osmr/research-papers/1992/ec921690.htm) · [Settlement rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Cost-sensitive classification

- **Published, high.** Elkan's Bayes decision rule chooses the action with the smallest conditional expected cost. The cost matrix can be expressed as regrets relative to the best action in each truth state. [Elkan 2001, §2](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~elkan/rescale.pdf)
- **Interpretation, high.** For the insurer, set wrong acceptance cost to `min(y,c)` and wrong rejection cost to `0.5 y`. The latter is incremental because a correct acceptance of a fair charge already costs `y`. The team submits a shared threshold, which constrains the classifier's available actions. A fairness classifier can also estimate the outgoing charge's chance of earning a rejection premium. [Derived from slides 3–4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, high for the objective and medium for the fit.** Calibrate `P(T<y | X,A_j=y)`, prioritize calibration near the cap-adjusted cutoff, and score the chosen `b` in euros. For incoming charge data, test out-of-round payment against the one-third baseline. [Elkan 2001](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2001/133) · [Settlement rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### First-price auction bid shading

- **Published, high.** A first-price buyer trades off winning probability against the surplus left after payment. For symmetric risk-neutral bidders with independent uniform private values on `[0,1]`, Vickrey derives `bid(v)=(n−1)v/n`. [Vickrey 1961, homogeneous rectangular case and appendix I](https://cramton.umd.edu/market-design-papers/vickrey-counterspeculation-auctions-and-competitive-sealed-tenders.pdf)
- **Interpretation, high.** The transferable part is optimizing money times probability against a distribution of thresholds. Here the team sets a selling price `a`, each opponent settles independently by the rules, and fair rejection increases revenue. The first-price coefficient and its dependence on `n) have no basis in this game. [Derived from slides 3–4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md) · [Vickrey 1961](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1961.tb02789.x)
- **Transfer hypothesis, medium.** Compare aggressive and shaded `a` values using simulated opponent limits and posterior fair-value draws. For `b`, use the charge distribution directly. Its direction and role differ from a buyer's auction bid. [Settlement rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Reserve prices and monopoly pricing

- **Published, high.** In Myerson's symmetric regular independent private-values model, an interior seller reserve with zero value for retaining the object solves the zero-virtual-value equation `r−(1−F(r))/f(r)=0`. The same equation follows from differentiating a single buyer's posted-price revenue `r(1−F(r))`. [Myerson 1981, §§5–6](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/campuspress.yale.edu/dist/3/352/files/2011/01/1981-optimal-auction.pdf)
- **Interpretation, high.** If the charge is certainly inflated throughout a comparison set and `a≤c`, the handyman's receipts are exactly `a P(B≥a)`. If all opponents reject, the same objective shape uses the fair-value survival `P(T≥a)` and a 1.5 multiplier. An insurer's `b` is an upper limit and trades capped fraud leakage against a fair-charge penalty. [Derived from slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, medium.** Use the two survival objectives as checks for extreme opponent profiles. For certainly inflated charges above the cap, expected revenue is `c P(B≥a)` and is weakly decreasing. The full payoff grid handles mixtures and point masses. [Myerson 1981](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.6.1.58) · [Settlement rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Online posted prices and contextual pricing

- **Published, high.** Kleinberg and Leighton study regret from sequential posted prices with binary purchase feedback. Their random-valuation theorem assumes a fixed distribution and a regular revenue maximum. Javanmard and Nazerzadeh learn a structured contextual model from binary observations with regularized maximum likelihood and prove regret bounds under conditions on the choice model. [Kleinberg & Leighton 2003, Theorem 1.2](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rdk/papers/oppa.pdf) · [Javanmard & Nazerzadeh 2019](https://jmlr.org/papers/v20/17-357.html)
- **Interpretation, high.** An accept bit at `a` tells us `B_j≥a`. A reject bit tells us `B_j<a`. The resulting demand curve is one input to handyman revenue, alongside expert fairness. A fair rejection can pay more than an acceptance. For the insurer, incoming charge amounts estimate where a change in `b` would matter. [Derived from slides 3–4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, medium.** If acceptance bits are exposed, fit a monotone survival model across a few normalized price levels, pooling by type. For an observed bit `z`, maximize `z log S(a|X)+(1−z)log(1−S(a|X))`. Score `a` using the game rule and test a small neighboring candidate only where uncertainty is material. Game contexts, opponents adapting, and payment ambiguity leave the published regret rates unverified here. [Kleinberg & Leighton 2003](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rdk/papers/oppa.pdf) · [Javanmard & Nazerzadeh 2019](https://jmlr.org/papers/v20/17-357.html) · [Rules, slides 4 and 7–9](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Ultimatum bargaining and take-it-or-leave-it offers

- **Published, high.** Güth, Schmittberger, and Schwarze tested one-off proposer demands followed by accept/reject decisions. Straub and Murnighan found that proposers made lower offers and responders accepted lower offers when responders did not know the total being divided. [Güth et al. 1982](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0167268182900117-) · [Straub & Murnighan 1995](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/016726819400072M)
- **Interpretation, high.** For each pair, `a` is a take-it-or-leave-it amount and `b` encodes a cutoff chosen before seeing opponents' offers. The written game rules assign a 1.5× payment on fair rejection and settle every pair. Those mechanics determine the offer trade-off. [Rules, slides 3–5 and 7](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, low.** Allow observed opponents to exhibit different thresholds for obvious exclusions, ambiguous scope, and clean priced items. Compare this with a common normalized threshold model. Experimental human fairness preferences supply a question to test and no bot-population parameters. [Güth et al. 1982](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0167268182900117-) · [Rules, slides 2–4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Winner's curse and common-value selection

- **Published, high.** Milgrom and Weber analyze affiliated bidder signals and condition expected value on own and rival signals when deriving bidding strategies. The value conditional on the event that makes a bid win can differ from its value conditional on own information alone, as in Wilson's model of bidding with disparate information. [Milgrom & Weber 1982](https://web.stanford.edu/~milgrom/publishedarticles/A%20Theory%20of%20Auctions%20and%20Competitive%20Bidding.pdf) · [Wilson 1969](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.15.7.446)
- **Interpretation, high.** Opponent acceptance or rejection can be informative about `T` if bots use unmodeled private evidence about the case. Incoming high charges may reflect a high fair-value estimate, aggressive bidding, or both. There is no single winning bid per line, so the direction of the selection effect is empirical. [Derived from slides 3–5](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, medium.** Compare `P(T≥a | B_j≥a,X)` with `P(T≥a | X)` on labeled cases. For the insurer, test whether the incoming charge and opponent improve calibration of `P(T≥y | X,A_j=y)`. If so, use joint scenarios or these conditional probabilities. [Milgrom & Weber 1982](https://web.stanford.edu/~milgrom/publishedarticles/A%20Theory%20of%20Auctions%20and%20Competitive%20Bidding.pdf) · [Settlement rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Costly state verification and inspection games

- **Published, high.** Townsend derives incentive-compatible contracts when a report is cheap and verifying the true state is costly. Avenhaus et al. model strategic inspection, including simultaneous and sequential versions. Blocki et al. model an auditor committing to inspection probabilities and a punishment level before an auditee chooses a response. [Townsend 1979](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-09/Optimal%20Contracts%20and%20Competitive%20Markets%20with%20Costly%20State%20Verification.pdf) · [Avenhaus, von Stengel & Zamir 1996](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0377221795002618) · [Blocki et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.0356)
- **Interpretation, high.** Rejecting is the inspection-like action. Detection saves the avoided capped payment, and inspecting a fair line costs the additional 0.5×. `T` is adjudicated for settlement. Both teams submit simultaneously and the penalty is fixed. The bot's scarce resource is analysis time before submission. [Rules, slides 3–4 and 7](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, medium.** Spend extra inference time where resolving coverage or price uncertainty could change the chosen `a` or `b` and move a material amount of money. For a signal `Z`, estimate its value by `E_Z[max_a R(a|Z)−min_b C(b|Z)] − [max_a R(a)−min_b C(b)]` before accounting for latency. Use ordinary simultaneous best responses unless the official feedback reveals public commitment before future rounds. [Townsend 1979](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0531%2879%2990031-0) · [Blocki et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.0356) · [Rules, slides 3–4 and 7](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Insurance claim leakage and invoice auditing

- **Published, high.** Dionne, Giuliano, and Picard study a constrained audit strategy based on observable fraud indicators and apply it to a European insurer's data. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services checks documentation, coverage, coding, and billing in its payment audit program. Its improper-payment rate measures compliance and does not establish intent. [Dionne et al. 2009](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1080.0905) · [CMS CERT program](https://www.cms.gov/data-research/monitoring-programs/improper-payment-measurement-programs/comprehensive-error-rate-testing-cert) · [CMS CERT explanation](https://www.cms.gov/sites/default/files/2022-02/CERT_vs_MAC_Fast_Fact_co_branded.pdf)
- **Interpretation and exact mapping, high.** Model `T` as zero when coverage or relation fails, and estimate the conditional gross line price when both pass. The game uses “fraud” as the binary condition `a>T`. Its score counts capped payments on inflated accepted charges and a premium on fair rejected charges. Both can be costly even if a binary accuracy metric looks good. [Rules, slides 2–6](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, medium.** Estimate `q(y)=P(covered ∩ related | X) P(T≥y | covered ∩ related,X)` for `y>0` under the stated zero rule. Extract policy section, damage relation, quantity and unit, VAT, and price evidence as traceable inputs. Allow ambiguity in coverage or relation, then send the resulting joint fair-value distribution to both optimizers. [Dionne et al. 2009](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1080.0905) · [Rules, slides 2–6](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Tax compliance and audit selection

- **Published, high.** Allingham and Sandmo model reporting when audit probability, sanctions, and taxpayer utility affect incentives. Kleven et al.'s Danish randomized audit experiment found substantially less evasion with third-party reporting than with self-reporting. A U.S. Government Accountability Office review distinguishes detecting a discrepancy from measuring the resulting revenue gain and identifies selection problems when nonresponse is treated like confirmed noncompliance. [Allingham & Sandmo 1972](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/course/Allingham%26SandmoJPubE%281972%29.pdf) · [Kleven et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA9113) · [GAO 2024](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106126)
- **Interpretation, high.** Explicit policy and documented quantities are closer to checked evidence than a rival's self-reported charge. A team can receive a fair rejection windfall even when the rejecting team's behavior came from a missed deadline. Such outcomes measure payments and do not independently establish opponent price knowledge. [Rules, slides 2–4 and 6–8](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, medium.** Track whether `b` reduces euros lost on verifiable exclusions and expensive ambiguous lines. Track `a` returns against opponents with confirmed missing submissions separately in analysis. Use all revealed expert-labeled lines to estimate case-type priors and stratify validation if settlement reveals only selected labels. [Kleven et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA9113) · [GAO 2024](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106126) · [Rules, slide 8](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

### Repeated games, fictitious play, and no-regret learning

- **Published, high.** Robinson proves convergence of empirical fictitious play for a fixed finite two-player zero-sum matrix game. Hart and Mas-Colell's regret matching approaches the set of correlated equilibria in finite games. Cai et al.'s polymatrix result makes the marginals of a coarse correlated equilibrium Nash in the fixed zero-sum polymatrix model. Auer et al.'s EXP3 learns a finite set of actions from their individual observed rewards under bounded adversarial rewards. Arora et al. show that no bandit algorithm guarantees sublinear policy regret against an adaptive adversary with unbounded memory. [Robinson 1951](https://cs.brown.edu/courses/csci1440/labs/2023/lab01/robinson.pdf) · [Hart & Mas-Colell 2000](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/~hart/abs/adapt.html) · [Cai et al. 2016](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/cai/publication/cai-zero-sum-2016/cai-zero-sum-2016.pdf) · [Auer et al. 2002](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0097539701398375) · [Arora et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.6400)
- **Interpretation, high.** A fixed-table replay can compute best responses to empirical opponent actions, with one histogram for charges and another for limits. Tournament cases and opponents' estimates change. Treat the fixed-table convergence results as controls for offline analysis. An aggregate score provides one joint reward, while role-specific feedback makes separate policy updates possible. [Cai et al. 2016](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/cai/publication/cai-zero-sum-2016/cai-zero-sum-2016.pdf) · [Game spec](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/research/raw/GAME_SPEC.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, medium for modeling and low for generic exploration.** Start with smoothed empirical or censored opponent distributions conditioned on price scale and case type. Compare expanding-window and recent-window estimates by prequential scoring. With sufficiently frequent role-specific rewards and known bounds, test a small menu of whole policies, such as the saddle and alternative model-based best responses. Estimate regret against a declared set of policies on the same observed stream. [Robinson 1951](https://cs.brown.edu/courses/csci1440/labs/2023/lab01/robinson.pdf) · [Auer et al. 2002](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0097539701398375) · [Arora et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.6400)

### Additional transfer — censored data and selective labels

- **Published, high.** Turnbull estimates a distribution from grouped or interval-censored observations. Bartók et al. distinguish losses from feedback signals in partial monitoring. Lakkaraju et al. show why evaluations can be biased when prior decisions determine which outcomes become observable. [Turnbull 1976](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/38/3/290/7027379) · [Bartók et al. 2014](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.2014.0663) · [Lakkaraju et al. 2017](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/kdd17-selective.pdf)
- **Interpretation, high.** Accepted/rejected outgoing offers can censor `B_j`. A revealed fairness label at `y) gives `T≥y` if fair and `T<y` if inflated. Combine informative signals for the **same** line into one interval. For positive `y≤c`, an accepted payment of `y` reveals no fairness label. If `0<c<y` and the per-pair payment is exact, the four payments `y,c,1.5y,0` identify all four outcome cells. [Derived from slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)
- **Transfer hypothesis, medium.** Use exact labels where available and fit interval observations with likelihood mass `P(L≤T<U | X)` if the observation process supports that likelihood. Validate separately on unbiased labels or by modeling why labels were observed. Log the available feedback schema before selecting a model. [Turnbull 1976](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/38/3/290/7027379) · [Lakkaraju et al. 2017](https://www.kdd.org/kdd2017/papers/view/the-selective-labels-problem-evaluating-algorithmic-predictions-in-the-pres) · [Rules, slides 4 and 7](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md)

## Recommended strategy hypotheses

Use paired replay on the same cases when `T` and opponent actions are available. Compare net euros, gross receipts and payments, and downside by round. Keep all lines from one case together. With weaker feedback, compare only quantities it identifies and label scenario results as simulations. The numerical cap, input grid, and results schema are the first dependencies to verify in official materials. [Rules and API, slides 4 and 6–9](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md) · [Selective labels](https://www.kdd.org/kdd2017/papers/view/the-selective-labels-problem-evaluating-algorithmic-predictions-in-the-pres)

| Priority and hypothesis | Concrete test | What would refute it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Separating coverage and damage relation from gross pricing improves both roles. **Medium.** | Compare a policy-and-damage model with an amount-only model on withheld case labels and paired settlement. [Rules, slides 2–6](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md) | Coverage and relation signals fail to improve the zero-value probability or cause a net loss from denying fair lines. |
| 2. Cap-aware prefix optimization improves insurer payments. **Medium for a material gain.** | Compare with a lower one-third-quantile limit. Include high-charge cases and scenarios with informative opponent bids. [Rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md) | The cap never binds and the posterior has a single crossing, or the fitted prefix rule raises out-of-round insurer payments. |
| 3. Expected-receipt pricing beats a fixed conservative quantile. **Medium.** | Compare outgoing payoff with the same fair-value posterior using fixed quantile, pure saddle, and joint best response. [Rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md) | Grid optimization yields lower out-of-round receipts, or estimated permissive limits vanish when tested on new cases. |
| 4. The pure saddle is a competitive starting benchmark. **High under common information, medium for the live field.** | Simulate against missing, conservative, and permissive opponents with the same posterior. Then compare on labeled live replay if exposed. [Saddle proof](#pure-saddle-on-a-shared-grid) · [Rules, slide 4](/Users/sebastianboehler/Documents/GitHub/START/claim-to-fame-challenge-deep-dive.md) | Material feedback shows actions predict `T` beyond the bot's features, or a different amount grid or cap rule invalidates the guarantee. |
| 5. Pooled contextual estimates beat per-team fits early. **Medium.** | Predict each new round before observing it. Compare pooled and team-level models on acceptance, charge prediction, and replay euros where identifiable. [Javanmard & Nazerzadeh 2019](https://jmlr.org/papers/v20/17-357.html) | Persistent, resolvable team effects improve held-out expected payments after accounting for case type. |
| 6. Adaptive exploration can recover its early score cost. **Low without itemized feedback.** | Compare a small, predeclared exploration budget with the fixed posterior policy in case-sequence simulation and then in authorized live play if feedback supports it. [Kleinberg & Leighton 2003](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rdk/papers/oppa.pdf) · [Arora et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.6400) | Only aggregate scores appear, acceptance has too little variation at legal amounts, or plausible upside is smaller than exploration losses. |
