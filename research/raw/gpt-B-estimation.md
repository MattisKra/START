Working assumption for this brief: German pricing in EUR, standard 19% VAT where applicable, and the scoring in slides 2–8. ## TL;DR

Ranked for the hackathon. Confidence refers to the recommendation's applicability here.

1. **Submit a valid baseline early. High.** Aim for the first acknowledged PUT by second 25 and a final update by second 54. A missed round sets both values to zero and charges the insurer 1.5× for each rejected fair bill. The slides allow resubmission and provide a non-persisting Game 0. [Challenge slides][slides]
2. **Preserve the invoice index, unit and tax basis. High.** Compute gross line totals with decimal arithmetic. Test indexes across pages, German decimal formats, daily equipment rates, flat fees and VAT before adding model calls. [Challenge slides][slides] · [PyMuPDF text and tables][pymupdf] · [SIRADOS prices][sirados]
3. **Give exclusion and unrelated work their own probability at exactly €0. High.** Ask for the governing clause, any exception, and the causal link for each line. Price the work conditionally on eligibility. [Challenge slides][slides] · [InsClaimBench, §2][insclaim]
4. **Use the payoff to choose the acceptance limit. High.** Under a common posterior and a nonbinding cap, accept positive charges while `P(t < x) ≤ 1/3`. That gives a lower-third threshold, usually below the median. A binding cap changes the rule. [Challenge slides][slides]
5. **Anchor repair prices in local, scope-matched references. Medium–high.** Prioritize water-damage services and trades. Cache activity, unit, region, date, VAT and bundle contents with each reference. Add public quotes for auto glass and exact-SKU goods. [BKI Altbau][bki] · [SIRADOS prices][sirados] · [Kienle][kienle] · [Carglass exchange][glassreplace] · [Miele Germany][miele]
6. **Choose charge `a` independently of `b`. High on the distinction.** Start with the charge that maximizes `x × P(t ≥ x)` over plausible amounts. Replay full game payoff as soon as there is credible information about opponent acceptance. [Challenge slides][slides] · [TacTex09, §§3–8][tac]
7. **Keep the first model batch small. Medium.** Run a fast structured case pass while local price lookup runs. Compare GPT, Claude and Gemini in parallel for expensive or disputed lines only when the deadline permits. Model agreement is an uncertainty signal. Game calibration is **unverified**. [Price quantile study, §§5–6][vedula] · [OpenAI latency guide][latency] · [Self-consistency study][self]
8. **Inspect the meaning of feedback before updating. High.** Exact expert values enable calibration. Known charges plus fair/inflated labels give bounds. Accept/reject alone measures the chosen threshold and cannot identify the expert value. [Challenge slides][slides] · [Turnbull][turnbull] · [Adaptive conformal inference][aci]

## 1. Price estimation and retrieval

### What the evidence supports

- **Published result, high confidence within the study.** Vedula et al. trained Mistral-7B with quantile heads on Amazon product, used-car and boat prices. Table 2 reports median-estimate mean absolute percentage errors of 16.86% for Amazon and 6.30% for used cars. The few-shot models lagged the fine-tuned model in their comparisons. Figure 2 also shows that more examples can eventually degrade Amazon results. **Interpretation:** quantile learning can help where clean price labels exist. **Transfer hypothesis, medium for output shape and low for accuracy:** request conditional quantiles and use a few closely matched examples. The study does not establish zero-shot calibration or estimate German repair invoices. [Vedula et al., Tables 2–3 and Fig. 2][vedula]
- **Published result, moderate confidence in the demonstration.** A 2026 construction study connected Claude to building-model quantities and the Craftsman national cost database. It reports 42.3 ± 3.7 seconds across five warm runs of one US electrical case. Manual checks found a receptacle overcount and conduit-length undercount. **Interpretation:** separate quantity extraction, reference matching and deterministic costing. **Transfer hypothesis, medium:** cached reference matching is actionable. Five warm runs on one case do not establish our tail latency or line accuracy. [Construction demonstration, abstract and §§4–5][bim]
- **Published result, high confidence within task scope.** Wang et al. found gains from sampling and majority aggregation on arithmetic and common-sense tasks. **Interpretation:** this tests agreement on tasks with discrete answers. **Transfer hypothesis, low:** independent model passes could catch scope mistakes. Shared references and shared model biases can produce correlated errors. A GPT + Claude + Gemini majority, or three sampled prices, has **unverified** predictive coverage on this game. [Wang et al.][self]
- **Published example, medium confidence within scope.** The Insoore AI paper describes an Italian motor-claims pipeline that detects damage, identifies the affected part and uses market labor and specific parts to estimate costs. **Interpretation:** photos can help choose the repair operation and parts. **Transfer hypothesis, medium:** use a VLM to tell repair from replacement or narrow the visible part. Blind photo-to-EUR accuracy on German claims is **unverified**. [Insoore AI, methodology][insoore]

## 2. Posterior, payoff and calibration

### From line evidence to a distribution

**Proposed model, high confidence in the decomposition.** Let `D` be the case evidence, `p₀ = P(t = 0 given D)`, and `F₊(z)` the predicted cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the gross total given `t > 0`. For `z ≥ 0`, set `F(z) = p₀ + (1 − p₀) × F₊(z)`, with `F₊(0) = 0`. Convert each unit-price quantile to a gross line total before pooling. If the unit or operation is genuinely ambiguous, retain the plausible matching scenarios with explicit weights. Estimate `p₀` jointly from coverage and relatedness, or multiply the probability of coverage by the probability of relatedness given coverage. This avoids assuming the two are independent. This is a working forecast distribution from elicited priors until checked against expert values. [Challenge slides][slides] · [InsClaimBench, §2][insclaim] · [Price quantile study][vedula]

### Convert the distribution to bids

**Derivation, high confidence given slide 4.** For any positive incoming charge `x`, set `s(x) = P(t ≥ x) = 1 − F(x⁻)` and `m(x) = min(x, c)`. The left limit matters when `t = x` has mass. Acceptance costs `xs + m(1 − s)` in expectation. Rejection costs `1.5xs`. Accept if `m(1 − s) ≤ 0.5xs`, equivalently `P(t < x) ≤ x / (x + 2m)`. If `x ≤ c`, this is `P(t < x) ≤ 1/3`. For example, when `x = 1.5c` the cut is `3/7`, approximately 42.9% fraud probability. With a common posterior and a nonbinding cap, use the largest attainable cent or candidate charge meeting the inequality, with `b = 0` when every positive price fails. A zero atom above one-third thus puts `b` at zero in this case. Break indifference ties consistently. [Challenge slides, slides 3–5][slides]

**One submitted acceptance limit, high confidence in the method.** With a binding cap or if an incoming charge carries information about `t`, individually optimal accept/reject decisions can be non-monotone in `x`. With a credible opponent charge distribution, scan the sorted charge grid. Start with rejecting everything and add the incremental cost `m(1 − s) − 0.5xs`, weighted by the mass at each charge. Choose the prefix with the lowest total cost. Use `s(x) = P(t ≥ x given D and the opponent charged x)` if available. Start with the nonbinding-cap formula while the cap and opponent data are unknown. [Challenge slides, slides 3–5][slides]

**Charge selection, high confidence in the payoff and medium in the proxy.** Against a randomly selected opponent with acceptance limit `B`, maximize the expected receipt.

`x × P(t ≥ x, B ≥ x) + 1.5x × P(t ≥ x, B < x) + m(x) × P(t < x, B ≥ x)`

Evaluate on a grid of actionable amounts. Simulate common case-level `t` and plausible opponent `B` jointly when there is evidence, since both may follow the same report. With no opponent model, start with `x × P(t ≥ x)`. This is a lower bound on expected receipts under the payoff table and gives a concrete default with no opponent inputs. Relative to a very aggressive quote it can sacrifice upside when opponents routinely accept inflated prices. [Challenge slides, slides 3–5][slides]

**Worked check.** Suppose, for illustration, `P(t = 0) = 0.20` and the positive value is uniform on €300–€700. The nonbinding-cap rule gives `b = €366.66` on a cent grid. The conservative receipt objective is maximized at `a = €350`. Different values arise from different payoffs even with the same forecast. [Challenge slides, slides 3–5][slides]

### Estimate and recalibrate uncertainty

- **Published result, high in scope.** Romano et al.'s conformalized quantile regression adjusts fitted lower/upper quantiles with scores from a held-out calibration set. It gives finite-sample marginal interval coverage under exchangeability. Gibbs and Candès adapt the coverage level using observed coverage errors and prove a long-run frequency result under distribution shift. **Interpretation:** coverage guarantees apply to the defined prediction set and assumptions. Check zero-mass and CDF calibration separately from central interval coverage. **Transfer hypothesis, medium if expert labels arrive:** use one-sided calibration around the operational lower-third cutoff, with round-level validation. [Conformalized quantile regression][cqr] · [Adaptive conformal inference, §2][aci]
- **Published result, high in scope.** Tian et al. found verbalized scores often beat conditional probabilities for several RLHF models on three QA benchmarks. Groot and Valdenegro-Toro observed frequent overconfidence in their language and vision-language evaluations. **Interpretation:** elicited probabilities are model features. **Transfer hypothesis, low without labels:** ask about a specific event, such as `t=0` or `t>=€400 gross`. Once there are enough labels, fit a regularized monotone recalibration or a single logit offset. Check reliability near the decision threshold. Raw confidence and model vote fractions carry no game-calibration guarantee. [Tian et al.][tian] · [Groot & Valdenegro-Toro][groot]
- **With exact expert values, proposed.** Save predictions before outcomes. Recalibrate the zero probability using a Brier score or log loss on `1[t = 0]`. For positive prices, first fit a pooled log-median residual correction. Add scale or trade-specific corrections only if supported out of round. For the full `b` rule, estimate one-sided errors using `b_raw − t` on **all** labeled lines including zeros. For nominal two-thirds marginal coverage, take the order statistic at rank `ceil((n + 1) × 2/3)` on `n` held-out exchangeable scores and subtract it from the new raw lower bound. If the order exceeds `n`, the finite-sample rule yields a trivial bound. Round down to nonnegative cents. Split or resample by round or template. Exchangeability of individual neighboring rows is **unverified**. Report the fraction with `t < b`, pinball loss near `q₁⁄₃`, CRPS for the full distribution, and realized or simulated net payoff. Observe the equality convention when computing misses. [CQR][cqr] · [Vedula et al., evaluation][vedula] · [Gneiting & Raftery][scoring] · [Challenge slides][slides]

### Online updates depend on what the scoreboard exposes

| Observable feedback | What to update |
| --- | --- |
| Exact `t` per line | Score forecasts before each update. With sparse data, update one shared offset or coverage level, and keep a fixed baseline for comparison. For a lower bound, an ACI-style update is `α_next = α_now + γ × (1/3 − miss)`, with `miss = 1[t < b]` and small step `γ`. A miss moves the next bound to a lower quantile. Update only where that indicator is observable. If only some labels surface, track selection and avoid claiming population coverage. [Adaptive conformal inference][aci] · [CQR][cqr] |
| Known `x > 0` and trustworthy fair/inflated bit | A fair charge gives `t ≥ x`. An inflated charge gives `t < x`. Intersect all informative results for one line into `[L, U)`, keeping the endpoint convention and treating contradictory logs as an error. Maximize `log(F(U⁻) − F(L⁻))` across distinct lines for one strongly regularized correction, such as a common positive log-price shift. Use this only to the extent that the threshold and disclosure mechanisms are accounted for. A lack of thresholds near zero confounds the zero mass and the positive lower tail. [Challenge slides][slides] · [Turnbull][turnbull] |
| Known `x > 0`, decision, and exact pairwise payment | A rejected charge with payment `1.5x` is fair. A rejected charge with payment zero is inflated. An accepted charge is distinguishable from its payment only when a known cap satisfies `c < x`, in which case payments `x` and `c` identify fair and inflated. Discard ambiguous cases and log how labels became observable. [Challenge slides, slide 4][slides] |
| Only accept/reject, or aggregate standings | On an outgoing known charge `a`, acceptance proves `B ≥ a`, and rejection proves `B < a`. Use the logged probes to estimate opponent thresholds where observed, subject to selection. Your own accept/reject reveals the relation between your `b` and the opponent charge. Neither provides an expert-value label by itself. With standings only, freeze truth calibration and use offline replay. [Challenge slides, slides 3–5][slides] |

**Limits, high confidence on identification.** For threshold feedback, a simple alternative to the line-level interval fit is to check Brier/log loss for `P(t < x)` on observed fair/inflated outcomes, weighted so a heavily queried line cannot dominate. This calibrates outcomes at the observed thresholds under the logged mechanism. It does not validate a full posterior on unseen charges. Model the selection or report the observed subset when rejection or opponent information affects which labels surface. Conformalized survival analysis provides guarantees for particular right-censoring assumptions. Our two-sided, strategically selected bounds require separate justification. Approximate online calibration performance for this game is **unverified**. [Turnbull][turnbull] · [Conformalized survival analysis, abstract][survival] · [Challenge slides][slides]

## 3. Policy coverage, relatedness and photos

- **Published result, high on benchmark reporting.** The October 2026 InsClaimBench preprint evaluates six LLMs across 3,780 auto, property and health cases. Joint decision-and-amount accuracy ranges from 47.54% to 73.15%. The best atomic-rule accuracy is 95.48%, while the best exact rule-vector match is 36.90%. **Interpretation:** a strong clause check can coexist with a wrong final decision. **Transfer hypothesis, medium:** extract clause-level findings, check exceptions and causation separately, then apply eligibility and price arithmetic in code. Benchmark accuracy here is **unverified**. [InsClaimBench v1, §§2–4][insclaim]
- **Industry evidence, medium.** Verisk's XactXpert runs carrier estimating rules at the line level and markets alert-driven claims-leakage reduction. Its page cites nearly 10,000 claims without enough methods there to establish a transferable effect size. Tokio Marine describes a Shift deployment that checks estimates, policy/claim data and photo consistency for claim handlers. Debener et al. evaluate supervised and unsupervised claims-fraud prioritization against investigation outcomes. **Interpretation:** production workflows emphasize rule checks and triage. **Transfer hypothesis, medium:** use scope/rate/duplicate checks as cheap signals for review. A duplicate may be legitimate across rooms and a missing history field provides no signal. [Verisk XactXpert][xactxpert] · [Tokio Marine and Shift, pp. 3–5][shift] · [Debener et al., Table 5][debener]
- **Published result, high on task scope.** CarDD provides 4,000 images for detection and segmentation across six car-damage categories. **Interpretation:** image labels describe visible damage. **Transfer hypothesis, medium:** ask a VLM for image ID, visible object/room, damage type, agreement or contradiction, and `not_observable`. A bathroom photo can corroborate visible flooring damage, but may show neither the cause of the leak nor the full area. Send selected images for specific high-value ambiguities. Missing, blurry or cropped photos should leave evidence unresolved. [CarDD][cardd] · [Tokio Marine and Shift, p. 4][shift]
- **Proposed schema, medium.** Per invoice index, record `coverage: covered|excluded|unknown`, `relatedness: related|unrelated|unknown`, cited policy clause and exception, damage-report span, affected asset/room, operation, `p_zero_raw`, and optional photo observations. Distinguish the damage source from resulting damage and mitigation. A drainpipe repair and resulting wall drying can have different coverage. Verify every quoted span in the input. Give decisive exclusions or contradictions high zero mass when supported, and preserve uncertainty for ambiguous lines. A GDV sample policy can supply practice examples. The round's policy controls the decision. [Challenge slides][slides] · [InsClaimBench, §2][insclaim] · [GDV VGB 2016 model wording][gdv]

## Risks and unresolved inputs

- **Wrong target.** Expert `t` may differ from a current quote because of date, locality, scope or policy. Confirm market location, attachment language, deductible/depreciation treatment and whether partial eligibility affects a mixed line. Until verified, show the pricing basis in the log and keep reference uncertainty wide. [Challenge slides][slides] · [BKI FAQ][bkifaq] · [Destatis][destatis]
- **Correlated failure.** A misread row can give three models the same bad inputs. Validate row identity, unit, cited policy evidence and reference scope before agreeing on price. Check expensive rows first. [PyMuPDF text and tables][pymupdf] · [InsClaimBench][insclaim] · [Price quantile study][vedula]
- **Timeout cascade.** Sequential calls, high-resolution document uploads, OCR, rate limits and retries can consume the deadline. Measure the worst representative PDFs and cold requests. Always retain time for the required PUT. [OpenAI latency guide][latency] · [OpenAI PDF input behavior][pdfinput] · [PyMuPDF FAQ][pdfocr] · [Challenge slides][slides]
- **Feedback overfit.** Many opponents may expose thresholds for one line while there is still only one underlying `t`. Treat each round as a group and keep a stable baseline. Without truth-bearing data, any improvement in game calibration is **unverified**. [Challenge slides][slides] · [Turnbull][turnbull] · [CQR][cqr]
- **Organizer details to confirm.** Numeric cap `c`, permitted price/precision limits, line index conventions, exact result fields, and whether missed/partial PUTs and overwrite semantics match the slides. Confirm with official materials or Game 0 within the fair-play rules. Keep opponent learning disabled until the feedback semantics are known.
