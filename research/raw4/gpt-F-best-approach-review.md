## 1. Decisions from first principles

Let `T` be fair gross line value, `C ≥ 4T` the shared cap with a positive floor, `a` our charge, `b` our acceptance limit, and `A_j, B_j` an opponent's charge and limit. For a charge `x` against limit `y`, the transfer is

```text
pay(x, y; T, C) = min(x, C)             if x ≤ y
                 1.5x                  if y < x ≤ T
                 0                     otherwise

line net = Σ_j pay(a, B_j; T, C) − Σ_j pay(A_j, b; T, C)
```

For expected euros, `a` and `b` optimize separately even if opponent prices and limits are correlated. Cases can have shared uncertainty across lines. Gross amounts are the inputs to both actions, with VAT and justified quantity applied once. **[opened]** [Game rules](../../materials/GAME_DESCRIPTION.md)

### Known value and the role of the cap

- **Reviewer.** `b = T` always attains the minimum payment. Any cutoff between the highest fair and lowest inflated received price also works for that realized field.
- **Issuer.** A fair charge earns `x[n + 0.5·#(B_j < x)]`. That expression rises through `T`, so `T` is the best fair charge. Over all prices, compare it with `min(x,C)·#(B_j ≥ x)` for `x > T`. The maximum inflated-price revenue occurs at a willing reviewer's limit or at the cap if within a willing interval. A single unlimited reviewer with `C > 1.5nT` suffices to defeat the fair maximum. `C ≥ 4T` is a **lower bound**. Four permissive reviewers is no universal break-even, and a limit of four times our estimate gives no guarantee about clipping. At `T=0` a positive charge can mathematically win a payment. I would submit zero where the documents support exclusion, consistent with the issuer's stated role and the jury's honesty criterion.
- **Under uncertainty.** For features `X` from the case, maximize `Σ_j [a·P(a≤B_j|X) + 0.5a·P(B_j<a≤T|X) − E[(a−C)_+·1{a≤B_j}|X]]`. Silent reviewers contribute `1.5a·P(T≥a|X)`. More silence raises the value of getting the fair charge right. The optimal amount also depends on how active acceptance changes around the fair-value boundary.
- **Reviewer with information in the charge.** Accept an incoming `x` if `E[min(x,C)|X,A=x] ≤ 1.5x·P(T≥x|X,A=x)`. For uncapped payments the boundary is `P(inflated|X,A=x) ≤ 1/3`. If a known cap is below `x`, the boundary is `1−C/(1.5x)`. Given our documents alone and an uninformative charge, the usual one-third quantile follows. When the field shades its estimates, the incoming price is evidence. The evidence is weaker when everyone makes the same mistake. We commit one cutoff before observing charges. If the posterior is non-monotone, minimize the **sum of expected euros** over cutoffs, including zero, using the modeled charge density. A crossing of 1/3 is sufficient only in the monotone, uncapped case.

For scale, compare charging at a posterior median with charging at 80% of it. With `log(T/m) ~ N(0,.30²)` and inflated revenue set to zero, the expected factor `a·P(T≥a)/m` is 0.50 at the median and about 0.62 at 0.80. With an exact value the optimum is 1.00. If two credible scenarios put the same received charge on opposite sides of `T`, accepting risks a loss of `x` over the oracle and rejecting risks `0.5x`. Taking the higher of two proposed limits is no minimax rule. Resolve the scenario from evidence, or use its posterior loss and the resulting cutoff.

### Synthetic sensitivity check

I ran 20,000 standard-normal draws with seed 1729 and a 0.01 multiplier grid. Set `T=1, C=4, m=exp(sd·Z)`. The eleven reviewer limits were `s` zeros, `10−s` at 0.88, and one at 1.15. Incoming prices were `s` zeros, `8−s` at 0.70, plus 0.92, 1.07 and 1.28. Sum the transfer above for each draw. Reviewer regret is payment above the cost at `b=T`. Units are multiples of `T) summed across opponents. The example uses assumptions only.

| Silent | Log-price error SD | Best `a/m` | Revenue at best | Best `b/m` | Regret at `b=m` | Regret at `b=1.25m` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 0.10 | 0.86 | 11.57 | 0.94 | 0.37 | 1.53 |
| 5 | 0.30 | 0.70 | 8.33 | 0.84 | 1.01 | 1.45 |
| 0 | 0.10 | 0.88 | 10.48 | 0.94 | 0.37 | 1.53 |
| 0 | 0.30 | 0.72 | 7.11 | 1.00 | 1.22 | 1.50 |

In this example reducing price error from 0.30 to 0.10 gains over 3× line value in issuer revenue. It also reduces reviewer regret. This illustrates the value of price information and why the cutoff depends on the field. It supplies no tournament forecast. With perfect documents and symmetric rational peers, `(a,b)=(T,T)` is a Nash equilibrium. Shared reading mistakes can move the field far away from it.

### Rank, variance and the 30% jury

The leaderboard component is `(70/11)·Σ_j P(our final net > peer j's final net)` in expectation, with a tie term once the organizer's rule is known. A €1 transfer from a rival to us improves that pairwise gap by €2 and each other pairwise gap by €1. For a large remaining schedule, use robust expected euros and reduce correlated failures across expensive lines. In the final 5–10 games, simulate pairwise gaps under each candidate policy. Increase variance only if it demonstrably raises the sum of attainable rank probabilities. An uncatchable gap, a comfortable lead, and several clustered peers can give different choices. A portfolio with no wagered bankroll does not call for Kelly sizing. Use the option-barrier analogy to price lost fair income and use a risk reserve for large unresolved coverage or price errors. **[opened]** [Evaluation criteria](../../materials/EVALUATION_CRITERIA.md)

## 5. Recommended operating policy

### Case reader and calculator

1. **Parse first.** Extract all invoices and their positions, trade, printed unit and quantity. Keep a short evidence table from the description and case-specific policy provisions, with exact source spans. Record document hashes and which optional schedule facts are missing. Start with the full current wording on a new edition. The first call is Luna low, text only, through the new schema with the minimal additions needed for evidence and separate components.
2. **Use an ordered set of operations.** Produce a gross price distribution from labour, parts, materials and justified attendance, or use a stated equivalent purchase or repair price with its scope. Apply verified fractions, cost-head caps, class caps, and deductibles in the order the policy specifies. An explicit euro amount and a soft description anchor are different inputs. Fuse soft signals in log space with uncertainty. If estimates share a source, downweight one or keep alternative scenarios. When a cap binds, apply it to every draw, in the stated order, before deriving line medians and decisions. Once a deductible or cap is applied, verify accounting for the entire group. Allow a bounded list of operations such as proportion, minimum, rateable cap, and one deductible. Avoid arbitrary program generation or execution.
3. **Validate scope.** Reject a candidate payload with missing, duplicate or unmapped positions, quantities outside documented units, non-finite amounts, a twice-applied VAT rate or deductible, or a broken hard limit on the fair-value calculation. A documented upper bound on `T` also bounds `b` for the disciplined policy. For `a`, round down to cents where a hard bound is used. Confirm that the terms responsible for a large shift have matching spans in the policy and description. If models disagree, prefer the evidence-backed scenario and combine amounts only where scope and units match.

### Initial actions to freeze for shadow replay

| Evidence class | Charge `a` | Acceptance limit `b` |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit exclusion or clear absence of required optional agreement | 0 | 0 |
| Exactly established payable amount | `T` rounded down to cents | `T` rounded down to cents |
| Narrow line amount with a verified source and deterministic allocation | `0.97m`, or the documented floor if lower | `m`, with any hard ceiling applied |
| Ordinary covered line with a market price estimate | `0.80m` | `m`, with any hard ceiling applied |
| Material unresolved scope or amount | Evaluate zero and `0.65m,0.80m,0.90m` over both plausible scenarios. Prefer the smaller exposure on near ties. | Evaluate zero, `0.85m,m,1.10m` against the scenarios and the field. Choose the cutoff with lowest expected euro cost within the documented exposure ceiling. |

Here `m) is the median conditional on valid scope, after coverage arithmetic. The scenario calculation still accounts for the probability of zero and uses the first-principles payoff. Start with three possible correlation and field regimes in shadow: current active, five defaults, common pricing error. Keep the ordinary multipliers frozen while learning. Revisit a common price offset once at least 15 consecutive post-freeze games include several like-for-like lines. Shadow-test a half-strength offset capped at ±0.08 in log price, applied before both decisions. A quote cannot be “narrow” just because it was scaled to a shared cap. Avoid a giant or extreme quote sourced only from one market-price guess. An example validation gate is any line representing at least 40% of the case's estimated value with a price ratio outside 0.67–1.50 between independent cost and claim estimates. Have the second reader resolve scope before accepting such a revision.

### 60-second schedule and reliability

| Time from server release | Action |

| T+14–30 s | Escalate only a material hazard: conflicting coverage, missing schedule, operation-order ambiguity, quantity or tax mismatch, or a cost-check discrepancy above 1.5×. | T+30–45 s | Compare evidence and downside. Replace only with a validated improvement and a complete payload. Keep the prior acknowledged payload on an uncertain or late revision. |

One normal call, at most two automatic calls. Use a separate provider for failover where the setup has been tested. A good answer can stand as soon as it is acknowledged. Consecutive failed or late payloads should trigger a rollback to the known working path before the next round. A supervisor and one single-writer lock prevent accidental concurrent last-write-wins overwrites. A timeout on a write can be ambiguous, so retry the same payload before allowing any revision. For a fresh high-risk change, preserve the current deploy while executing the shadow harness through the actual timed receipt path using local fixtures.

## Ideas I would try that nobody on the team has mentioned

- **Estimate a bill as a parts-and-labour basket before reading policy amounts.** A blind component estimate for each expensive combined line gives a second source of price information. For the frost fitting, estimate one attendance, actual fitting and labour, then incremental imaging. This targets the common error seen in all three readers. The policy reader then maps components to cost heads.
- **Audit counterfactual disagreement by failure mode.** One paired table can tell whether two policies diverge because of missing evidence, a fairness cliff, reviewer acceptance censorship, or a deadline. Spend effort on the high-euro ambiguity rather than maximizing the number of scored lines.
- **Use invoice grouping and scope identities as constraints on uncertainty.** Sample shared attendance and deductible once. Use line ratios to allocate a binding class cap in each sample. This captures negative correlation between capped line amounts and prevents a confident output on every line from summing to an impossible bill.
- **Make the first path price-blind on exclusions.** Extract item purpose and covered share before requesting a price. Show the second reader only the disputed fact and cited source passages. This protects against rationalizing a large price after reading an attractive total or decorative photo invoice.
- **Delta-based update budget.** Only replace an acknowledged payload when new evidence lowers estimated downside or adds value above a small, fixed case-scale hurdle. Keep a decision log with the quote and arithmetic that earned the update. This cuts the incentive to overwrite just because another model finished.
