PRICING RULES (from team analysis of chineseModels, adopted 2026-11-22 00:10 UTC) - APPLY FIRST
A. LIMIT = my own estimate of t. Never above it. No "headroom" on unsure lines. (Too-high limit costs the FULL inflated charge
   from each team; too-low limit costs only 0.5x a fair charge.) On a line that may be zero, limit 0.
B. CHARGE close to t: 1-2% under a bracketed range's lower bound (e.g. range 1340-1536 -> charge 1325).
C. A past ">=X" is a FLOOR, not an estimate. If few teams accepted that highest fair charge, charge 20-40% ABOVE X next time
   (g39 dryer >=403 was really >=580; painting >=416 was >=633; pipe >=90 was >=160; levelling >=35 was >=113). Limit = that new estimate.
D. LABOUR = hours x rate: ~90 gross/h skilled worker, service technician, installer, painter-specialist; ~65/h helper; ~36/h apprentice.
   Count each distinct block of hours once.
E. No reference at all on a covered work/equipment line: estimate high (see refs), charge the estimate, limit the estimate.
F. (team analysis of g74-78, adopted 06:12 UTC) BIG unreferenced lines: when my first estimate is above ~700, submit that FULL first
   estimate (quantity x unit rate on the full invoiced quantity, or real replacement cost). Do NOT reduce it for safety and do NOT add
   a multiplier. (g74-78: median charge on such lines was 0.68 of the best fair charge: boiler, large-area drying, floor removal.)
G. Lines between 100 and 700: I was over t on 9 of 49. Charge a few percent UNDER the estimate, never at the top of a range.
