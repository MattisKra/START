Burglary at the policyholder's home, and a fuller claim on this one. The front door was forced overnight and a range of things were taken from the ground floor and the bedroom - clothing, several pieces of jewellery, and some odds and ends.

The clothing was mostly the usual wardrobe: three pairs of jeans, five shirts, two knitted jumpers, a pair of leather ankle boots and a pair of trainers, all ordinary high-street labels. Among it was a winter coat bought two winters ago for EUR 289 that had only been worn twice since, the last winters having been mild. The settlement agent also put in for a premium designer jacket to replace the stolen one, which was a mid-range waterproof jacket from a high-street outdoor brand.

The jewellery included several everyday pieces - a thin 9-carat gold chain, a pair of silver hoop earrings, a silver charm bracelet and a steel fashion watch - none of them ever listed anywhere. The one high-value piece is a ring that does sit on the policy's scheduled-articles endorsement: it was added four years ago at what it had cost the year before, EUR 6,500. There was also a claim for an antique brooch, though there was no receipt, photo or valuation for it. The schedule shows a valuables limit of EUR 1,000 per item and EUR 3,000 per event, and no deductible.

On top of the property, the agent listed personal mileage to and from the police station and time spent putting the claim together.

A locksmith boarded up and re-keyed the forced door the same night, and also cleaned up the muddy footprints the intruders had tracked through the hallway.

The police attended and there is a crime reference.
