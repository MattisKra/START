The pool house claim, and this one came with a lot of paperwork. Indoor pool at the villa, a supply-pipe connection separated while the owners were away, and the water spread from the pool room into the conservatory and down through the floor into the plant room underneath.

Alongside the pool pipe repair and the drying and reinstatement, there was the usual scatter of extras on the invoices. An inflatable pool float had drifted into the conservatory and knocked over a few plant pots, and both the float and the pots ended up billed for replacement. The plant-room electrics had some components swapped that were genuinely water-affected and a few more done as a precaution. The big rug in the conservatory was soaked through and went off to be cleaned and dried.

For what it is worth, the schedule on this one has the loose-furnishings extension ticked and none of the other optional add-ons; the owners said they had looked at the pool equipment cover at renewal and decided against it.

There is also some noise to watch in the billing - a couple of the crews each charged for debris disposal, the leak-detection firm added a second call-out, there is catering for the work crew on one invoice, and someone repainted the garage next door even though the water never got near it. Flagging all of it for the file. The owner also upgraded the floor to a high-end natural stone. Nobody has sent anything showing what was down there before.
