Townhouse, three levels, escape of water from upstairs. Policyholder rang it in on the Sunday night, I went out on the Monday morning with the plumber already on site.

Background first. The whole family had left on the Friday afternoon for a christening up north and stayed two nights with the in-laws. Heating was left running on the low setting, the boiler and radiators were fine when I checked, and it was mild for January that weekend. Nobody else had a key; the neighbour only waters the plants in summer.

Source is the small guest WC on the top floor. The plumber took the cistern apart and found the fill valve stuck open - the seal on the float side had worn through and it just kept filling and spilling. Nothing frozen, nothing cracked, it simply failed. He put a new standard valve in the same day. His reading is that it had been running since some time on Saturday.

From the WC the water went across the floor, soaked the vinyl and got into the floor build-up underneath, and the skirting round the WC is swollen along its foot. It then ran through the floor structure towards the stairwell. The stair carpet itself was damp at the edges only and has dried out on its own, they are not claiming for it. Below the stairs it came out through the plasterboard under the flight and down the wall beneath it into the living room - see the first photo, stains and blistered paint on both. Moisture readings were taken in every room on the way down, and the plumber's sheet has the WC floor, the stairwell side, the living room ceiling and wall under the stairs, the living room floor and the hobby room ceiling all reading wet. The master bedroom and the kitchen read dry.

The living room is oak herringbone parquet, about twenty years old, and the new blocks going in are the same oak and the same pattern. The odd part of this one is the robot vacuum. It lives on its dock in the living room. The owners showed me the app on their phone: no schedule is set, and the log has a single run starting at 04:12 on the Sunday that nobody started from the phone or the button. It drove through the puddle under the stairs and kept going, so the wet patch on the parquet ended up a lot bigger than the drip zone, with a track leading off towards the rug in front of the sofa. The edge of the rug nearest the stairs was wet through when they came in.

The vacuum was found stopped in the middle of the floor with water on top of it and a red light, and it would not charge or start again afterwards. They had an electrician look at it; water in the board, not worth repairing. They have already bought a new one.

The rug is a loose woven one, not fixed down. It went off to a specialist cleaner, who has confirmed it comes up fine with a wash and a slow dry, so it is coming back rather than being replaced.

On the skirting: the boards in the WC and the swollen run in the living room were taken off before the floor came up. The few lengths along the wall where the vacuum track ran were swollen at the bottom too and had to come up with the parquet itself, they broke as the blocks were lifted. So more new skirting went in than was taken off as a separate job.

Downstairs is the hobby room, in the basement directly under the living room. The ceiling there is plasterboard and it came down in a patch over the billiard table, second photo. The water landed on the table: the cloth is soaked and stained, the cues in the rack and on the table got wet, and the pendant lamp over the table filled with water and one shade cracked. The table frame looks sound to the eye but they had it taken apart and checked anyway. Shelves, books, the old stereo and the tools on the pegboard just needed wiping down. Concrete walls down there, nothing done to them.

Drying ran for about two weeks, with a separate firm doing the fans and the checks, and the plasterers finished with filling and a clean-up of the dust and the bits of ceiling. No hotel, they stayed in the house throughout.
