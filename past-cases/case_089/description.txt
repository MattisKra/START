Water damage in the cellar, notes for the claim.

The tiles along the back wall of the storeroom were wet one Monday morning and the stain was climbing the plaster above them. Handy Hans was booked anyway for the shelving, so he arrived at eight and spent the first part of the morning hunting for the leak himself: he pulled the skirting, tapped along the wall and opened two of the tiles, but could not find where it came from. The leak detection people came out after lunch with a thermal camera and a listening probe and pinned it to a split press joint on the copper cold main behind the lining. Hans then cut the lining open along the pipe, moved and propped the shelving, and fixed the cut edges and loose battens with screws and tape before he left at four.

The plumber came the same afternoon, on his own, from one until seven. That main is the thick 28 mm copper line that feeds the whole house. He cut out the bad section and put in new copper with press fittings. It has to go up and round the meter niche, so there are five right-angle bends in it. Three thin branch lines came off that stretch, the old 15 mm tails to the laundry, the outside tap and the boiler room, and each was reconnected to the new main with a transition piece.

The storeroom measures 6 m by 5 m, and the first moisture readings only showed that room as wet. A dryer went in there, and the soaked mineral wool behind the lining along the pipe came out.

Four days later the storeroom was drying down, but the hall at the foot of the stairs had started reading wet under the tiles. The plumber came back the next morning and was there from eight until half past twelve. The fitting just past his new section was weeping too. He replaced that stretch with new 28 mm copper as well, this time following the slope of the wall, so all five bends in it are 45 degree ones. After that the drying covered the whole cellar, which is 10 m by 5 m with the storeroom included. A second dryer was brought for the hall, and a fan was set up next to the dryer in the storeroom, pointing at the open wall.

The blue lay-flat hoses and the extra blowers stacked by the wall in the photo were left by the drying firm when they collected the second dryer and were never connected.

Once the readings were normal the tiler came once and stayed from seven in the morning until nine at night to relay the loose floor tiles and the two wall tiles Hans had taken off. The owner still had a box of the original tiles from when the cellar was done, so only adhesive and grout had to be bought.

The brown streaks on the wall in the first picture are from the leak. The ring folder on the table is the owner's paperwork for the claim.
