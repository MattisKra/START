Cellar leak, back room of the basement.

It started as a wet patch creeping across the floor tiles at the foot of the plasterboard partition. Nothing was visible, so the leak detection company came first and narrowed it down to a press joint on the grey 16 mm composite cold line behind the boards. Handy Hans then took the boarding off the partition along its length so the pipe could be reached, moved and propped the shelving, and fixed the loose battens and cut edges with screws and tape.

The plumber came that afternoon with his helper. He cut out the bad joint and put in a copper section in 15 mm with press fittings. It has to dog-leg round two studs and the window reveal, so there are five bends in it, with a transition piece at each end back onto the grey line. While the line was open they ran a hose as a temporary bypass so the washing machine could still be used. That line is just the thin cold feed to the laundry corner, nothing bigger was opened.

The first moisture map only showed the back room as wet, about 30 m2. A dryer went in there, the wet wool came out of the stud bays, and about a square metre of the insulation board glued to the outside wall behind the pipe came off as well.

A few days later the back room was drying down, but the front part of the cellar where the stairs come down had started reading wet under the tiles. The plumber came back and pressure-tested the line. The next joint along, about two metres further on and past his first transition, was weeping too. He replaced that stretch the same way, again five bends, and joined it straight onto his own copper, so only one more transition was needed. The elbows were a different model this time because the wholesaler had run out of the first ones. Drying then covered the whole cellar, about 50 m2. The dryer was wheeled out of the back room into the middle of the cellar, and a blower was set next to it pointing at the wall.

The second yellow box by the shelving in the photo is the owner's own dehumidifier from the laundry corner. It runs most of the winter anyway and is not on anyone's invoice.

Once the readings were normal the tiler relaid the floor tiles that had come loose in both parts of the cellar. The owner still had two boxes of the original tiles from when the cellar was done, so only adhesive and grout had to be bought.

The black marks high up on the outside wall are old. That wall gets condensation every winter and has looked like that for years.
