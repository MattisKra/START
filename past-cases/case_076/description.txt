Cellar leak, laundry side of the basement.

First sign was a dark line in the grout at the foot of the plasterboard partition between the laundry and the store room, and the floor tiles along it started to rock. No pipe was visible anywhere, so we called the leak detection firm before anyone opened anything. They traced it with their gear to a joint on the cold line inside the partition, roughly in the middle of its run.

Handy Hans came the next morning and stayed the whole working day, from half seven until four with a half-hour lunch break. He took the boards off the lower half of the partition so the pipe could be reached, and late that afternoon, once the plumber had signed off his test, he put them back, screwed down the loose battens and taped and filled the joints. The screws, tape and filler came out of his van.

The plumber came out that same morning with his apprentice, as soon as the pipe was open. The old line was too far gone to just remake one joint, so he cut out the bad length and put in a 28 mm copper run with press fittings. It has to go round the corner of the partition, under the window reveal and back up to the old route, so there are five bends in it. He used one transition piece at each end onto the existing pipe and a third where the short 15 mm branch to the washing machine tees off. They left the new run under pressure at lunchtime, and he drove back on his own in the middle of the afternoon to read the gauge and sign it off before the boards went back on. The apprentice only carried and held things, the plumber did the pressing and the test.

The moisture survey marked the laundry as wet: 6 m along the partition and 5 m to the outside wall. One dryer went in for that area, and the wet mineral wool came out of the stud bays and was bagged up.

The drying firm dropped off more kit than they used. The second dryer by the shelving, the stack of blowers and the blue hoses in the photo stood there unplugged the whole time and were collected with the dryer at the end.

When the readings were normal the tiler came with his journeyman in one van and the two of them worked a full seven-hour day relaying the floor tiles that had come loose along the partition. We still had spare boxes of the original tiles in the store room, so he only had to buy adhesive and grout.

The sign on the hatch and the folder on the table are from the loss adjuster's visit.
