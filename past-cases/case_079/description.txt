Kitchen-diner on the ground floor at the back of the house, a bit under 30 m2 including the table corner.

First sign was the vinyl lifting in front of the sink and a damp smell from the sink cabinet. Water was getting behind the units and under the floor and nobody could see where from, so a leak detection firm came out. Two of them, the engineer and his mate, spent most of the day on it. They metered the floor and the wall, opened the board in a couple of places and got through a lot of tracer gas, plugs and caps isolating sections of pipe. They found two points. The tee under the sink, where the braided tap hose meets the 22 mm copper tail, was spraying (second photo). Behind the wall, the 28 mm line had split at a soldered joint and had been running into the cavity.

The plumber then cut the board back to open the wall over the whole run. Around the split the 28 mm line between the two branches, about three metres, had sagged off its clips and was replaced, with a reducer at each end onto the existing 22 mm. The tail under the sink was redone over about a metre. The first photo shows the new copper in place with some of the black lagging already back on.

The foam lagging on the pipes in the wall was soaked and came off first, which was quick. The mineral wool filling the cavity behind the sink run was wet right through and had to be pulled out bay by bay, which took most of a day. New lagging later went onto the replaced 28 mm run and the rest of the exposed pipework.

The kitchen was dried with one condensation dryer and a blower (photo 1) until the final readings were back to normal. At the end they vacuumed the plaster dust, board offcuts and vinyl scraps off the kitchen floor. The vinyl and the swollen base of the sink cabinet are not in this batch because the cabinet maker is still quoting.

The kitchen table had been pushed up against the end of the counter next to the sink, and it stood in the water overnight. The legs swelled and splayed and the top opened at the glue joints. In the photo it is propped back in the dining corner, but it rocks and cannot be saved. It was plain pine, bought new about seven years ago, used every day and with the usual marks. The replacement is a similar plain pine table from the same kind of shop.

The roof space is over the bedrooms at the other end of the house. There is an old brown stain on the boarding up there from when the flashing round the chimney leaked two winters ago, and the owner asked the drying firm to set some kit up there while they were here anyway. The owner also had the carpenter look over the wooden bench and chairs that stay out on the patio all year, because they have gone grey and furry.
