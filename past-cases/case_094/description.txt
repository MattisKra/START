Basement leak at the policyholder's place. A water-carrying pipe in the basement sprang a leak, and the water spread through the walls and floor of the basement and up into the ground-floor room directly above, both of which came up wet on the moisture readings. A leak-detection crew traced the source before it was repaired.

The leak was in a 28 mm copper run under the basement ceiling. The plumber cut out the corroded stretch and rerouted it around a joist with press fittings, a handful of right-angle and half-angle bends, plus a few adapters where the new copper met the old smaller-bore lines. He came out twice: once to cut the line out and make it safe, and again a couple of days later to finish the new run and pressure-test it.

There was a fair amount of drying to do across the basement and that adjoining room upstairs, with the wet insulation pulled out first. Two dryer units were running at the same time, one down in the basement and one in the room above. A fan was run in the basement alongside the dryer unit, and while the crew was in the house they also ran a dryer in a first-floor bedroom that had not shown any sign of water.

The tiles that came up were in the basement, a patch of roughly 3.1 m by 3 m in front of the boiler. When the tiling went back, the owner chose premium large-format designer floor tiles, a clear step up from the plain basement tiles that were there before. Their choice, but a fair bit fancier than what came off. Apart from the tiles themselves, the tiler only needed adhesive and grout.

A bigger job than it first looked, spread across a few rooms.
