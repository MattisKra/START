Washing machine claim, utility room and hall.

The family were away skiing for the week and had left a load on the delay timer so it would be finished when they got back. Behind the machine, the little plastic hook that keeps the drain hose hung in the standpipe had snapped. When the machine pumped out, the hose dropped to the floor, and every rinse and spin went across the floor instead of down the drain. The neighbour who keeps a spare key saw water seeping under the front door two days later and turned the tap off under the sink.

The utility room is small and tiled, and it was wet from wall to wall. Most of the water then found the doorway and ran out over the oak boards in the hall for roughly two and three-quarter metres towards the front door before it soaked in. It was grey and scummy with lint and detergent, and it left a film on the tiles and the boards even after it had been mopped up. The post that had come through the letterbox was lying in it. The owner wiped down the big mirror in the hall himself; there's nothing claimed for that.

The drying firm came the next morning and took readings in both rooms. They shut the utility room door and ran their dehumidifier for the whole room. In the hall they only put drying mats on the wet strip of boards, because the rest of the hall read dry. Two of their technicians were there for about two hours each, setting up, taking readings and collecting the kit. The hall boards have settled flat again. The owner thinks a couple still look slightly cupped and says he may have the whole hall sanded and oiled next year.

Skirting in the utility room is painted softwood. It runs along the window wall (about 2.4 m) and the wall behind the machine (about 2.1 m). The other two walls have the doors, a frame and a cupboard plinth, so there's no real run there. Both runs had soaked up water and swollen at the bottom. In the hall the skirting went dark and lifted along both sides of the wet stretch and nowhere past it. It was all replaced in the same profile and finish.

The handyman hung the hose back in the standpipe with a new holder, then ran an empty programme while he watched the drain and the inlet connection. Nothing more came out. Since then the owner has bought an aquastop inlet hose himself. It isn't on any invoice.

The painting is an oil seascape in a heavy gilt frame. It hangs low on the hall wall a little way past the utility room door. The wall behind it drew water up, and the bottom of the canvas and the stretcher took on moisture, which left a tide mark along the lower edge. It belonged to the owner's late father, and an auction house valued it at around EUR 18,000 for probate in 2019. The owner is sure he mentioned it to the broker on the phone when they switched insurers, but the schedule on file lists no individual items, and nothing about the painting was ever sent in. The owners are private householders and pay the VAT themselves.

The family's usual decorator knew a restorer through a contact. The restorer collected the painting, examined it, dried and stabilised it, and then restored it. The decorator put the whole job through on his own invoice. The restorer's share runs to several thousand, and the restoration itself is by far the largest part of that.
