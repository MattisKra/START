Client's place has an open-plan ground floor, built in '94, original copper pipes. Over the years they'd basically worn out — not one dramatic burst, more like several spots along the run had gone porous at once. The plumber who fixed it said it's material ageing rather than a single accident.

Because it's open-plan, the flooring that got pulled up was pretty much the entire ground floor — little less than 100 sqm of linoleum, skirting included, all hauled out. 

Before that, there was a damage assessment call, then indoor leak detection with a technician on site for a few hours to pin down where exactly the pipe was failing.

Drying took the longest. Large-area drying covered around more than the floor removal number, since it reaches into wall bases and adjoining space too, plus extra treatment for enclosed under-floor cavities. Electricity draw for the dryers over the whole job came to about 2,800 kWh, they ran for quite a while.

The kitchen, which was part of the affected area, also got disinfected — about 100 m² there.
