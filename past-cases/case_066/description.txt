Indoor pool claim at the villa.

The family were away for ten days over New Year. The pipe that feeds the pool comes through the wall of the pool hall at about hip height, and the coupling on the end of it let go. Water kept pouring out of it until the housekeeper came in on her weekly check and found the floor under water. The pool itself was there when they bought the house and it is looked after by a pool firm on a contract.

The pool hall and the conservatory are really one room. The glazed folding doors between them have stood folded back for years, and the whole space is heated and ventilated together. Water covered the slate around the pool and carried on across the conservatory end. The inflatable ring the children had left out floated across and knocked over a row of citrus pots, so there was potting compost everywhere. The plants and pots aren't being claimed, and the wicker chairs dried out on their own.

The leak people found the coupling within the hour with their kit. They took moisture readings across the floor and walls, and came back on the third day to check them. Their fitter then spent the rest of that first afternoon remaking the coupling with new fittings. His hours and parts are on the same bill as the detection.

The plumbing firm wet-vacuumed the standing water first thing and set up the drying. Their rate sheet is a flat fee for setting up and attending the drying of the room, with the machines charged by the day on top. The big condensation dryer only stayed one day; after that the readings had dropped enough for them to move it to another job. One fan stayed until the third-day readings came back dry. The owner then ran his own dehumidifier for another week to be safe.

The pool end has bare rendered walls with no skirting. The render drew up a band about half a metre high along roughly twenty metres of wall and had to be made good; above that it stayed dry. The conservatory end is about four metres by three. It had painted softwood skirting all the way round its glazed sides and along the brick wall, and all of it swelled and split. It was replaced in the same softwood.

That end had plain grey ceramic tiles. When they came up, the owner had the floor done in natural stone instead, to match the slate around the pool. The drying firm thought the old tiles might well have survived a proper dry-out.

The big wool rug in the conservatory seating corner was soaked and had compost ground into it, so it went off to be cleaned and dried. The owner took the loose-furnishings extension for the conservatory when it was built, and it is on the schedule.

The light switch and double socket on the pillar next to where the pipe comes through were running with water, and so was the floor box for the lamp sockets in the conservatory. The building-services firm's electrician tested those circuits and passed them. While that firm was on site, their pool technician also looked over the pump and control unit, and since the sand filter was due its yearly service anyway, he did that as well. The same firm did the walls, the floor and the final clean, and charged one van.
