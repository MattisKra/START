Basement water damage.

The basement is a walk-out one: the house sits on a slope, so the back room has a glass door and window straight onto the garden at ground level. That back room, about 6 m by 5 m with a 2.4 m ceiling, is where a water-carrying pipe developed a leak at a joint. By the time we noticed, water had spread into the walls and across the whole floor of that room. The small boiler cupboard next door stayed dry; the boiler itself is from 2009 and was not touched.

On Tuesday 27 January a leak-detection firm came out with a thermal camera and moisture meter and pinned down the exact joint before anything was opened up.

The plumber came on Wednesday 28 January and spent the full day opening up, cutting out the bad section and remaking the joint with a few 90-degree copper press elbows and a transition piece where the old pipe changes size. He came back on the Thursday morning to pressure-test it and close everything up again.

Once the pipe was sorted the drying firm pulled out the soaked insulation and set up a single dryer in the room on the Thursday afternoon. It ran until they collected it on Thursday 12 February. We had moved the chest freezer and the boxes of Christmas decorations out to the garage beforehand.

The tiler then did the floor over two full working days, Friday the 13th and the following Monday, using plain standard grey floor tiles, the same kind as before.

The work was limited to that basement room. The invoices came as summarized trade items rather than fully itemized breakdowns.
