Cellar leak, utility corner under the basement stairs.

The owner found a puddle on the concrete plinth on a Sunday evening and shut the water off at the stopcock. The drip came from the brass compression nut where the horizontal cold line runs into the bend (second photo). The nut itself was sound, the olive inside had stopped sealing. The man from the drying firm undid the joint, fitted a new olive with some sealing paste, put it back together the same afternoon and fitted a new chrome cover ring where the line goes into the wall, because the old one cracked when the tiles came off.

About a metre of rock wool lagging on that bend was sodden. The owner had already peeled it off to see where the water came from, which is why the pipe is bare in the pictures. New lagging went back on afterwards.

The corner had a tiled panel, the only tiling anywhere in the cellar: plain white wall tiles from the cheapest range at the DIY store, and the same size went back. It ran about 2.4 m along the wall from the side wall, covered the wall to roughly 85 cm above the plinth and wrapped over the top and front edge of the plinth, another 40 cm or so. Water had got in behind it, the tiles sounded hollow wherever you tapped, and the whole panel came off. The render behind was damp over the same area and was hacked off. Only right around the bend had it gone soft down to the brick, roughly a metre square, and that patch was built back up before tiling. Photo 1 is the tiler setting cut pieces along the plinth.

Silicone only goes in the internal corner where wall meets plinth and up the end of the panel against the side wall, everything else is grouted. One hole had to be drilled through a new tile for the line. The tiler wiped the panel down when he finished.

Next to the corner is a plasterboard boxing on a timber frame that hides the meter. It was wet along the bottom, so the board came off and the mineral wool inside it went as well. The tiler bagged the wool and took it away in his van that day. The broken tiles, render and board went into the container the drying company had dropped off. The boxing is left open for now.

One construction dryer went into the corner. The first readings on the render and plinth were well above normal, the check a week later was back to normal and the machine was collected the next morning. The owner then kept his own small dehumidifier running down there for about another week, just to be safe. That one is not on any bill.

While the tiler was on site the owner also had him knock the loose plaster off the attic gable, which has had brown staining on it since the roof was re-felted a few years ago.
